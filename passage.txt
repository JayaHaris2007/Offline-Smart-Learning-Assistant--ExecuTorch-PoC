Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food. Plants take in sunlight through their leaves, which contain a green substance called chlorophyll. They also absorb water from the soil through their roots and carbon dioxide from the air through tiny openings called stomata. Using the energy from sunlight, the plant changes water and carbon dioxide into glucose, a type of sugar that gives the plant energy to grow. During this process, the plant also releases oxygen into the air. This oxygen is the same gas that humans and animals need to breathe. Without photosynthesis, there would be no food for most living things and the air would not have enough oxygen. Photosynthesis mostly takes place in the leaves, which is why leaves are usually flat and wide, so that they can catch as much sunlight as possible. Plants that grow in places with less light often have larger leaves to collect more energy.
